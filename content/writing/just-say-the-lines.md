> “For the heptapods, all language was performative. Instead of using language to inform, they used language to actualize. Sure, heptapods already knew what would be said in any conversation; but in order for their knowledge to be true, the conversation would have to take place.”
>
> ― Ted Chiang, *Stories of Your Life and Others*

An actor in a play knows the future (the script of the play) and even the lines of their counterpart. In this case, the things they say are not informative since both sides know what's coming. They are performative. The words need to be said for the play to progress and for the future to happen. The fact that both sides know them does not make them less necessary.

If you know the future, then your actions and speech are performative rather than informative. They make the future happen.

Really this is a different way of saying "fake it till you make it". You pick a future and you believe that it is inevitable. Now your job is to simply "read the script". Performative speech needs to be slightly reframed to include action. Now action is by definition performative but our definition will be "Something that is done not for the purpose of adding new information but just as a necessary plot mechanism". So when you take a risky action, you are not worried about the outcome, you are simply doing what you need to do to make the future happen.

Even an inevitable future still needs someone to read the lines.
